## What this means for Cloth (Assumption unless marked)
1. **Variants, barcodes, exchange and credit notes are already standard** at myBillBook, Vyapar and Busy. They are table stakes for us, not a reason to switch. Our phase 1 list (variants grid, barcode labels, exchange tracking, offline billing, GST bill) matches the market and no more.
2. **The master-plan claim "variants are thin in general tools" is not supported by this pass.** All three pages describe variant stock. Do not write that claim in landing copy. The remaining possible gap is season and festival forecasting, stuck-stock (sizes that do not sell) and reorder advice; I found none of these on the pages read (which only means the pages do not list them).
3. **Price.** The one INR price found for a garment plan is myBillBook Rs 3,490 to 3,990 a year (Rs 291 to 333 a month). Our draft Cloth ladder Rs 149 / 299 / 499 a month (Rs 1,788 to 5,988 a year) is not clearly cheaper. As with Mart, Cloth sells the AI and WhatsApp part (stuck-stock list, festival reorder suggestions, regular-buyer credit reminders), not billing price. The Mart proposal (free billing and stock tier, paid tier for AI and WhatsApp) applies here too (CLO-P1, not approved).
4. **Offline.** Vyapar describes offline billing on Android; myBillBook's page does not say. Offline billing is table stakes, and must be tested on a real phone before we claim it.
5. **Alterations and tailoring** (fabric by metre, stitching orders) are not on the pages read; the scope file keeps them as a Phase 2 idea. Keep it there until owners say they need it.

What this changes (Assumption):
1. **Price band of the incumbents for a small shop is now Rs 3,400 to 4,000 a year** (Vyapar, myBillBook), about Rs 285 to 335 a month. Our draft Cloth ladder (Rs 149 / 299 / 499 a month) sits at or below that only on its lower rungs. Billing alone will not win on price; the AI and WhatsApp part has to carry the sale (same view as the first pass). Cloth floor price still waits for D-23.
2. **Marg is a different segment** (pharmacy, grocery, bigger stores) at roughly 1.5 to 7 times the price; do not use it as the garment price reference.
3. Vyapar raised prices twice in two weeks of Jun/Jul 2026 (secondary source); the comparison table in the landing copy must carry a date and be re-checked before it is shown.
